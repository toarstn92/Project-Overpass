Self-help CBT techniques

Experiencing anxiety and unhelpful thoughts is a completely normal part of being human. Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) can help you understand yourself better and respond in more helpful ways.

CBT is usually done with a therapist, but you can practise many of the same ideas on your own. Our guides include practical CBT techniques to help you manage your thoughts, feelings and behaviours.

What is CBT?

CBT stands for Cognitive Behavioural Therapy. It's a type of talking therapy where you work with a trained therapist.

CBT is based on the idea that your thoughts, feelings and actions are connected. A thought can affect how you feel, and how you feel can affect what you do.

If you get stuck in a cycle of unhelpful thoughts, it can make you feel worse and lead to behaviours that keep the cycle going.

How 1 thought can impact your life

  1. You think, "no one likes me or wants to spend time with me".
  2. This makes you feel worried, sad, lonely or angry.
  3. Your feelings make you stop replying to messages from friends or avoid making plans.

This can make the thought feel even more true. Feeling more alone can reinforce the thought that no one wants to spend time with you.

CBT helps you recognise these patterns and learn practical ways to break the cycle.

Does CBT work?

Yes, cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) is one of the most effective talking therapies.

Research shows it can help with a range of mental health problems by teaching you practical ways to recognise and change unhelpful thoughts and behaviours.

It can help you improve:

  • anxiety
  • low mood
  • stress
  • sleep problems

For more support, including how to try CBT with a therapist, visit the NHS talking therapies and counselling page.
